Teaching notes, interpretation and limits

This mining demonstration: a bounded Apriori search counts sets of one, two and three distinct items. For each frequent set of size two or three, it generates every nonempty split into disjoint antecedent and consequent sets. A rule’s support is the support of their union. Results must meet both support and confidence thresholds. Rules involving four or more distinct items are not searched here; production tools can search much larger datasets and item sets.

Meaning of a rule: A ⇒ B means that when all attributes in A occur in a transaction, the attributes in B often occur too. It does not mean A causes B, and it does not guarantee B will occur. The two sides must be nonempty and have no items in common. A transaction is one basket; multiple quantities of an item do not count as extra transactions.

Support: the fraction of all transactions containing both sides of the rule. Confidence: the fraction of antecedent transactions also containing the consequent. Lift: confidence divided by the consequent’s overall support (its base rate). Lift above 1 indicates more co-occurrence than expected under independence; below 1 indicates less; 1 matches independence in this dataset.

Interpret cautiously: a popular consequent can produce high confidence without a meaningful association. Lift and confidence can also be misleading with very small counts. These metrics describe this dataset; they are not a statistical significance test and do not prove causation or future performance. Check the sample size, context and a separate dataset before making decisions. In other settings, basket membership can represent attributes such as a device type, a service used or an event recorded.
