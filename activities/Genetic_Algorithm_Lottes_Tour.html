Lotte’s tour of Vlakland

Start at home X, visit each required city exactly once and return home. Can a population of candidate routes evolve towards a shorter tour?

Follow the supplied four-city example, then search the full 20-city tour. Fitness = 1 ÷ total distance, so shorter tours have higher fitness. The goal is a good route, not a proof of the shortest possible route.

This walkthrough reproduces the PDF’s four starting tours, parent choice, A D crossover block and A/D swap. It breeds just the one illustrated child; the starting population remains visible for comparison. The mutation is deliberately chosen here. In the full search, mutations are random.

Why ordinary crossover can produce an invalid tour

The PDF’s ten-city example illustrates the danger of simply splicing chromosomes. Try different cut positions. Unlike order crossover, an ordinary cut may repeat cities and omit others.

The distance lookup from the PDF

Distances in kilometres. X is home. The same symmetric table is used in both modes. Every total includes X → first city and last city → X. The full search uses A to T; the guided example uses only A, B, C and D.

What each stage means

Population and chromosome

A population is a collection of candidate tours. Each chromosome lists the city visit order; a gene is one city position.

Fitness and evaluation

Add all route legs and take 1 ÷ distance. Shorter routes receive a higher score. Re-evaluate every new generation.

Selection

Choose parents using their fitness. The guided example uses the two best tours. The full search also offers tournaments to preserve more variety.

Order crossover

Keep a block from one parent. Fill the gaps using the other parent’s cyclic order, skipping cities already copied. Every city remains unique.

Mutation

Swap two city positions. This adds variation but is not guaranteed to help. A swap preserves the requirement to visit each city once.

Termination

After evaluation, check a target distance or generation limit. Return the best tour found, which may not be the globally shortest route.

Teaching notes and modelling choices

Based on the supplied PDF: the four-city walkthrough uses its four tours, fitness rule, selected parents, order crossover, mutation and 540 km target. It does not pretend that every random mutation will produce the illustrated improvement.

Full-search extension: populations begin as random permutations of A to T. The default selection repeatedly uses the two fittest tours, alternating which supplies the copied block. Tournament selection instead chooses the fittest of three independent draws for each parent, with replacement; a parent can be drawn twice. For each child, two distinct cut boundaries are sampled, the interval between them is copied, and the other parent is read cyclically from just after that interval. Mutation probability is applied once per non-elite child. Elite copies are an optional extension that preserves selected high-fitness tours unchanged.

Replacement and stopping: elites plus offspring form the next complete population. Only after that population is evaluated is the stopping rule checked. The target is checked before the generation limit. A separate best-seen record is kept even when elitism is zero. The guided example evaluates only its one illustrated child rather than inventing additional offspring not specified in the PDF.

Interpretation: there are 20! city visit orders with home fixed. Even treating each tour and its reverse as equivalent leaves more than 10¹⁸ possibilities. A genetic algorithm searches a sample of that space. Improvement can plateau; small populations or repeated selection of just two parents can lose diversity. More mutation is not automatically better. Neither reaching the target nor hitting the limit proves an optimal tour.

Reading the display: map positions approximate the PDF’s city plot, but all fitness calculations use its distance table. Gene colours indicate copied or mutated positions, not classes. The 20-city target and generation limit are demonstration settings, not values specified by the PDF. Automatic running pauses on loss of window or tab focus.
